How we read every week

Three circles of context

Before deciding what a verse means, we place it inside three circles, from closest to widest.

Literary

The verses around it, the book it sits in, and what kind of writing it is.

Historical

Who wrote it, to whom, when, and what was happening to them.

Big story

Where it fits in the whole Bible's story, from creation to Christ to new creation.

Observe→Interpret→Apply What does it say? What did it mean to them? What does it mean for us?

How (Not) to Read the Bible

A six-session series on reading Scripture in context, built on Dan Kimball's video series How (Not) to Read the Bible on RightNow Media. Each week we watch a session together, talk it over, then practice on real passages. Read the passages before we meet; handouts and slides go up as each session is ready.

    Homework

    Read Jeremiah 29:1–14 and answer these questions

    We ran out of time for this passage, so work through it at home with the three steps. Bring your answers to our next meeting. The same questions are in your student handout.

    Read Jer 29:1–14

    Before you start: Write what you've always thought Jeremiah 29:11 means.

    Observe
    1. Who wrote this letter, and to whom? (Verse 1.) Where are they living?
    2. What does God tell them to do in verses 5–7? Does that sound like a quick rescue?
    3. How long until God's promise is fulfilled? (Verse 10.)
    4. What were the false prophets saying? (Verses 8–9.)
    Interpret
    1. Who is the "you" in verse 11? One person or a whole people?
    2. Finish the sentence: The timeless truth here is that God…
    Apply
    1. If a friend posted Jeremiah 29:11 before a big job interview, how would you gently share what you learned?
